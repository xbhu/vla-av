# Use Case C1: Faithful-Reasoning Sensitivity Analysis (AutoVLA)

## Overview

This use case tests whether the natural-language Chain-of-Thought (CoT)
reasoning generated by AutoVLA (a Qwen2.5-VL-3B-based vision-language-action
model for autonomous driving, trained with GRPO on nuPlan/navsim) is
*causally necessary* for its trajectory decisions -- i.e., if you perturb
the CoT text, the instruction, or the perception input while holding
everything else fixed, does the decoded trajectory actually change?

Data: navsim mini subset, 138 scenes. Model: AutoVLA checkpoint
`AutoVLA_PDMS_89.ckpt`.

**Related**: see `usecaseC2_faithful_reasoning_action_coherence` in this same
cluster. C1 asks whether the CoT text is causally load-bearing at all; C2
asks a complementary question -- when the model *does* state an explicit
decision, does its content actually match what the trajectory does?
Together they give a fuller picture of CoT (un)faithfulness than either
alone.

## Design

For each scene, four conditions were compared against a shared baseline
generation (instruction and perception features held fixed unless the
condition itself perturbs them):

| Condition | Manipulation | Question |
|---|---|---|
| Noise floor (n=20) | Re-run the identical input twice | How much does the model's own sampling noise move the trajectory, with nothing changed? |
| CoT off | Prefill a "straightforward scenario" template that skips reasoning | Is the CoT text causally necessary for the trajectory? |
| Instruction counterfactual (cf1, cf2) | Swap the driving instruction to each of the other two classes | Does the trajectory respond to the stated instruction? |
| Perception masking | Blackout the front / front-left / front-right camera frames | Does the trajectory respond to visual input? |

Metric: `endpoint_l2` -- Euclidean distance (meters) between trajectory
endpoints under the two compared conditions. Wilcoxon signed-rank test
against zero for significance.

## Results (full battery, n=138)

| Perturbation | mean | median | p95 | Wilcoxon p |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Noise floor (n=20) | 0.009 m | 0 m | -- | -- |
| CoT off | 0.363 m | 0 m | 1.6 m | 4.6e-06 |
| Instruction cf1 | 2.617 m | 1.517 m | 8.95 m | 2.3e-23 |
| Instruction cf2 | 5.410 m | 2.836 m | 19.85 m | 3.4e-24 |
| Perception masking | 6.402 m | 4.154 m | 16.17 m | 2.2e-24 |

All four perturbations are statistically significant vs. the noise floor,
but effect sizes differ by more than an order of magnitude: instruction
and perception perturbations move the trajectory 7-18x more (in mean)
than CoT removal does.

**CoT-off distribution shape.** The gap between CoT-off's mean (0.363 m)
and median (0 m) is not incidental -- it is the signature of a
long-tailed distribution. Breaking it down: **116/138 scenes (84.1%)
show zero change** (trajectory identical to pointwise precision) when
CoT is switched off. Of the remaining 22 scenes, the displacement itself
has a secondary long tail (25th pct 0.14 m, median 0.52 m, 75th pct
1.64 m, max 21.05 m driven by a single outlier). Scenes with a genuinely
large CoT-off displacement (>1 m) are likely on the order of 3-5% of all
scenes, not the 15.9% the raw nonzero count suggests.

## Interpretation

Two different questions are being asked here, and they get different
answers:

- **Grounding** (instruction cf + perception masking): is the trajectory
  correctly sensitive to the true driving-relevant inputs? **Yes** --
  large, highly significant effects.
- **Causal faithfulness** (CoT on/off): is the generated CoT text a
  necessary causal mediator of the trajectory decision? **No, for most
  scenes** -- for 84.1% of scenes the trajectory is decoded identically
  regardless of what the CoT text says, as long as instruction and
  perception are unchanged.

These are not contradictory. The model is **grounded but unfaithful**: it
correctly responds to the real scene, but for most scenes the verbalized
"reasoning" it produces is generated in parallel with, rather than as a
causal input to, the trajectory decoding head. This matches the failure
mode documented in Lanham et al. (2023), "Measuring Faithfulness in
Chain-of-Thought Reasoning," which uses an equivalent truncate/perturb
methodology on text-only LLMs.

The non-zero 15.9% suggests faithfulness may be scenario-dependent (CoT
possibly load-bearing only for harder/ambiguous scenes), but this is a
hypothesis from the displacement distribution alone, not yet verified
against scene content.


## Limitations

- C1 establishes that CoT text is not *causally necessary* for most
  trajectories; it does not by itself establish whether the *content* of
  the CoT (when the model does state a decision) is accurate. See
  `usecaseC2_faithful_reasoning_action_coherence` for that question.
- Sample size is 138 scenes (navsim mini subset) -- large enough for the
  headline Wilcoxon tests to be robust, but the ~5 large-displacement
  CoT-off scenes rest on a small count and should be treated as a
  hypothesis (scenario-dependent faithfulness), not a confirmed finding,
  until checked against scene content or a larger dataset.
